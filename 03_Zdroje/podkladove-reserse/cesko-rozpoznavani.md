# Research: AI advertising in Czechia, recognizing AI use, labeling rules

Research date: 22 Sep 2026. Scope: Czech examples ("v našem okolí"), how to recognize possible AI use, labeling and regulation. Every claim below was checked against the opened source page (curl or archived copy). Czech quotes are verbatim.

Status legend: VERIFIED-PRIMARY = the originating body's own publication (brand blog, EUR-Lex, paper, platform newsroom). VERIFIED-SECONDARY = credible outlet quoting or reporting the fact (trade media quoting a brand, news investigation). UNVERIFIED = not confirmed in an opened source. CONTRADICTED = an opened source disagrees.

---

## 1. Summary

- **Czech AI ads are mainstream, not hypothetical.** Documented and brand-confirmed cases in 2025–2026: ePojištění.cz TV spot (Mar 2025), Rohlík Christmas spot (Nov 2025), FAVI (Dec 2025), T-Mobile T Fiber (Feb 2026), Baťa (Mar 2026), O2 Eurotel remake (Apr 2026), Jobs.cz (Apr 2026), Livesport virtual presenter AILU (Aug 2026), Penny "Tlapky hezky česky" (launched 21 Sep 2026, yesterday). [C1–C18]
- **Recommended main-slide examples (3–4):**
  1. **Rohlík, Christmas 2025**: fully AI (visuals, music, voiceover), one in-house author, ~80 hours, under €400 vs ~€300k the year before. Strong public backlash, last place in Médiář's weekly "Reklamní katovna" (35 %). Clear cost-vs-trust story. [C4–C8]
  2. **ePojištění.cz, 2025**: presented as the first Czech TV spot rendered by AI. Its brand manager later listed the AI's failures himself: US dollars instead of Czech crowns (added manually), steering wheel switching sides, human teeth and fingers on a chimp. Best "tells documented by the maker" source available. [C1–C3]
  3. **O2 "Více ze života" remake, 2026**: hybrid. The 1998 Eurotel spot was reconstructed frame by frame with AI, main actors filmed in a studio, music re-arranged with AI by the original composer Ondřej Soukup. Top-rated campaign of its Katovna round (60 %). Shows AI as a tool inside craft. [C9–C11]
  4. Optional: **Baťa, 2026**: AI visuals watermarked by the brand and built from real catalog photos so the shoes match the real product. Positive model of disclosure and product fidelity. [C13] Or **Penny, Sep 2026**: AI visuals plus real celebrity voices, the freshest example. [C14]
- **Consumer-harm counterexample:** e-shops using AI photos of products that don't exist (iROZHLAS/Radiožurnál, 31 Mar 2026). dTest reports dozens of complaints a month, and ČOI said it would check the e-shop. The side-by-side "ad vs what arrived" image is the clearest slide for a lay audience. [C23]
- **Political context (describe neutrally):** SPD's 2024 regional-election posters were AI-generated; the SPD spokesperson confirmed "všechny vizuály jsou vygenerované pomocí AI". The posters carried no AI label. A first-instance court fined SPD CZK 3 million for incitement (Jun 2026). The Prague Municipal Court overturned that ruling on appeal (final, 1 Sep 2026), and the prosecutor is considering an extraordinary appeal. The legal case concerned the content, not the use of AI. [C19–C21]
- **People are poor detectors.** A representative study of 3,002 people in the USA, Germany and China found image accuracy of 45.9–49.9 %, i.e. chance level. Germany, Czechia's neighbour, scored lowest at 45.90 %. AI faces were judged *more* trustworthy than real ones, and the least accurate people were the most confident. Large volunteer studies reach 62–76 %, still far from reliable. [C26–C31]
- **Czech public, May 2026 (ResSolution Group, n = 8,000):** 6 in 10 think they've seen an AI ad, but only 28 % are sure; 56 % expect brands to disclose AI; 40 % trust AI ads less. Note: this overlaps with the statistics agent's area. [C25]
- **Classic tells (garbled text, fingers, teeth, inconsistent details) still show up in Czech ads**, e.g. pseudo-text on Rohlík's milk carton that changes between frames. But sources warn they are fading: "Přepočítávání prstů na rukou už nemusí spolehlivě fungovat" (iROZHLAS, Mar 2025); OpenAI says GPT-4o "excels at accurately rendering text" but lists "Multilingual text rendering" as a limitation, which matters for Czech diacritics. [C32–C34]
- **Provenance tech exists but only proves presence, never absence.** C2PA Content Credentials are added by OpenAI (since 2024), Adobe, Google Pixel 10 cameras (every photo) and others. SynthID is Google's invisible watermark, and OpenAI added it to its images in May 2026. Public checkers: contentcredentials.org/verify, SynthID Detector / the Gemini app, OpenAI Verify. Metadata "can be stripped … by … screenshots" (OpenAI). None of the 13 images downloaded for this report carried C2PA. [C35–C37]
- **EU AI Act Art. 50 applies from 2 Aug 2026.** Deployers must disclose deepfakes, meaning realistic AI content resembling existing or plausible persons, objects, places or events. The Digital Omnibus (Reg. (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 Jul 2026) delayed only the *machine-readable marking* duty, to 2 Dec 2026, and only for generative systems already on the market. It did not delay deepfake labeling. The Code of Practice on marking and labelling was published 10 Jun 2026 and confirmed adequate; it comes with optional EU icons. Fines up to €15M or 3 % of turnover. [C46–C52]
- **Czech enforcement is still being set up.** ČTÚ is named as the supervisory authority (press release 31 Jul 2026). iROZHLAS (3 Aug 2026) says it will supervise "po schválení českého zákona o AI". I could not find the AI adaptation bill in the Chamber's bill list on 22 Sep 2026. [C53–C54]
- **Platforms:** Meta has required AI disclosure in political and social-issue ads since 2024. Meta and Google stopped political, electoral and social-issue ads in the EU in Oct 2025 because of the TTPA regulation. Meta labels ads made with its own gen-AI tools and, since 1 Jun 2026, auto-detects third-party AI via industry signals. YouTube and TikTok rely on creator disclosure plus C2PA reading. [C38–C45]
- **Czech self-regulation is thin.** The RPR Advertising Code only says AI must be used "s vědomím odpovědnosti" (art. 3.7), with no labeling rule, and I found no AI rulings. AKA recommends transparency toward clients (2023) and amended its ethics code on human oversight (May 2026). A lawyer at an AKA workshop (Aug 2026) explained that labeling depends on context: not every AI image is a deepfake, and platform auto-labels are not enough on their own. [C55–C58]

### Proposed 4-image "Guess the origin" activity

Order: 1 → 2 → 3 → 4. Show each image, let the audience vote AI / not AI / mix, then reveal the source. The lesson: appearance gives suspicion; the proof is always outside the image.

### Three guidance points: "podezření vs. doložení"

1. **Vzhled je jen podezření.** People score near chance on realistic images (45.9–49.9 %) and misjudge real photos too: in Kamali et al. some real photos were recognized as real by only 28 % of viewers. Tells are fading ("Přepočítávání prstů na rukou už nemusí spolehlivě fungovat"). A tell justifies a question, not a verdict. [C26–C34]
2. **Doložení = vyjádření autora nebo popis postupu.** Every solid Czech case here is proven by the brand or agency describing its workflow (Rohlík, O2, ePojištění, Baťa, Penny), usually in a press release picked up by MediaGuru, Médiář or MAM. Where the author is silent (the e-shop case), proof comes from outside evidence such as the delivered product, consumer organizations or regulators. Lawyer Petra Dolejšová advises agencies to archive their prompts as evidence. [C57]
3. **Technické stopy potvrdí, ale nevyvrátí.** An "AI info" label, Content Credentials or a SynthID hit is strong evidence. Their absence proves nothing: metadata disappears with screenshots and re-uploads, not every tool signs, and OpenAI says a "no signal" result can still be OpenAI content. Since 2 Aug 2026 a visible AI label on a realistic ad is a legal disclosure, but a missing label is not proof of human origin. [C35–C37, C46–C51]
