# AI ad campaigns and AI influencers: research notes

Research date: 2026-09-22. Scope: documented global AI ad campaigns (2017 to 2026), AI influencers and virtual brand personas, and the dropshipping hypothesis. Other agents cover models/platforms, history/statistics, and Czech examples.

Status labels in the claims table:
- VERIFIED-PRIMARY: read on the brand's, agency's, vendor's or platform's own page (or an artifact inspected directly).
- VERIFIED-SECONDARY: read in reputable press that I opened; the original was not accessible or not published.
- UNVERIFIED: seen only in search snippets or low-quality sources, or the source itself hedges ("reportedly").
- CONTRADICTED: another source gives a different figure or date.

"Maker claim" means the number comes from the brand, agency, studio or vendor, not from an independent measurement.

## 1. Summary

- Big brands now ship AI-generated TV and social ads routinely, but none of the documented cases is "one person with a prompt". Coca-Cola's 2025 spot still needed about 20 people at Secret Level by its founder's estimate, and McDonald's NL's pulled ad needed "Ten people, five weeks, full-time" per the production company's CEO.
- Speed and cost are the selling point. Kalshi's NBA Finals ad took two days and under $2,000 in AI generation costs (Kalshi's claim), but 300 to 400 generations to get 15 usable clips.
- Coca-Cola is the anchor case: 2024 remake of the 1995 "Holidays Are Coming" (Silverside AI, Secret Level, Wild Card), heavy backlash from creatives, then a 2025 follow-up with animals instead of human faces. System1's panel gave the 2025 ad its maximum 5.9-star score. Loud online criticism and measured viewer response can diverge.
- The clearest backlash case: McDonald's Netherlands "It's the Most Terrible Time of the Year" went online 6 Dec 2025 and was pulled within days. It carried an "AI generated" label on the end card (inspected), so disclosure alone did not prevent the backlash; the cynical concept was criticised as much as the AI look.
- Fashion is where AI replaces the photo shoot most directly: Mango Teen (July 2024, 95 markets, model fully AI, garments real), H&M digital twins of real models (first images 2 July 2025, labelled "Digital Twin" on the image), Guess ads in US Vogue August 2025 (AI model by Seraphinne Vallora, disclosed in small print, strong TikTok backlash).
- Undisclosed AI is the riskiest pattern: J.Crew (Aug 2025) posted AI "vintage" photos without disclosure, was exposed by Blackbird Spyplane, then added "Digital art by: @samfinn.studio".
- 2026: Svedka ran what it calls the first "primarily" AI-generated national Super Bowl spot (Silverside AI, ComfyUI). iSpot counted 15 of 66 Super Bowl ads (23%) "featured AI", most of them ads for AI products. Meltwater found nearly 50% of mentions of AI Super Bowl ads negative.
- Virtual influencers are older than generative AI. The best-known ones (Lil Miquela 2016, Shudu 2017, imma 2018, Lu do Magalu since 2003) are CGI/3D characters. Aitana López (The Clueless, Barcelona) and Mia Zelu are image-generator personas.
- Biggest brand-owned persona: Lu do Magalu, Magazine Luiza's character, 10M Instagram followers (seen 2026-09-22). Magalu says it rebuilt her in 2024 as a 3D model "preparada para o Unreal" with real-time animation and AI.
- Earnings claims come from operators only: Aitana "up to €10,000 a month", average about €3,000 (The Clueless via Euronews). Miquela's "over $10 million" is reported without a source.
- Consistent AI characters are made with reference-image features (Midjourney Character/Omni Reference, Google's Gemini 2.5 Flash Image "character consistency"), fine-tuning on a few images of one subject (DreamBooth, LoRA), or digital twins trained on photo sessions of a real person (H&M with Uncut, Mango's garment model).
- Trust data is mixed and mostly commercial: an agency survey says 76% of US/UK consumers trust virtual influencers for product recommendations (Billion Dollar Boy, n=4,000 consumers), while IAB found only 45% of Gen Z/Millennials feel positive about AI ads versus 82% of executives who think they do. NIM (3 × 1,000 respondents) found that labelling an identical ad "AI-generated" made people rate it less natural and less useful.
- Dropshipping hypothesis: there is solid evidence that AI avatar "UGC" ads are sold to and used by e-commerce sellers (Zendrop's AI Ad Generator for its dropshipping catalogue, Arcads, TikTok Symphony avatars) and that AI "doctors" and fake customers sell supplements at scale (NYT investigation, July 2026; 404 Media, July 2026). No source quantifies how many dropshipping stores use them, and no specific account is documented as a dropshipping operation.

### Recommended for main slides

Campaigns:
1. **Coca-Cola "Holidays Are Coming" 2024 → 2025**: the most famous AI ad; shows scale (claimed 10,000 frames, 70,000 clips), the human team behind it, backlash from creatives and a top pre-test score at the same time.
2. **Kalshi NBA Finals ad (June 2025)**: the speed/cost story in one number (two days, under $2,000 generation cost, 300 to 400 generations for 15 clips).
3. **McDonald's Netherlands (Dec 2025)**: pulled within days despite an "AI generated" label; the concept and the "AI slop" look combined into a PR problem.
4. **Guess in Vogue + H&M digital twins (2025)**: AI models replacing photo shoots, and two opposite disclosure styles (small print vs. "Digital Twin" on every image).
5. Optional opener: **Heinz "A.I. Ketchup" (2022)**, AI as the idea, not as a cost-cutter; positive reception.

Personas:
1. **Lu do Magalu**: brand-owned, openly virtual, 10M followers; shows the CGI + motion capture + AI pipeline.
2. **Lil Miquela**: the CGI pioneer, and her 2025 paid leukemia storyline for NMDP that split audiences.
3. **Aitana López**: fully AI-generated, operator income claims, team "plans her life" weekly and finishes images in Photoshop.
4. **Mia Zelu**: AI persona whose AI status sat behind a "Read More" button; many Wimbledon 2025 viewers took her for real.

### How consistent characters are made today

- **Reference image features.** Midjourney's Character Reference (`--cref`, Mar 2024) "tries to make the character match a 'Character Reference' image", "works best when using characters made from Midjourney images" and is "not designed for real people / photos" ([Midjourney](https://updates.midjourney.com/character-refs/)). V7 uses Omni Reference (`--oref`) "to put consistent characters and objects into scenes" ([Midjourney](https://updates.midjourney.com/omni-reference-oref/)). Google's Gemini 2.5 Flash Image (26 Aug 2025) lets you "place the same character into different environments", with an invisible SynthID watermark on every output ([Google Developers Blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/introducing-gemini-2-5-flash-image/)).
- **Fine-tuning on one subject.** DreamBooth (Google, 2022): "Given as input just a few images of a subject, we fine-tune a pretrained text-to-image model such that it learns to bind a unique identifier with that specific subject" ([arXiv 2208.12242](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.12242)). LoRA (Microsoft, 2021) is the cheap fine-tuning method creators use to train such "character LoRAs"; it cuts trainable parameters "by 10,000 times" versus full fine-tuning of GPT-3 ([arXiv 2106.09685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)). The paper is about language models; applying LoRA to image characters is common practice, but I found no operator of a named influencer stating they use LoRA.
- **Digital twins of real people.** H&M's twins were made by "feeding machine-learning algorithms multiple photos of models from different angles and lighting conditions" ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2025/07/08/hm-unveils-ai-generated-models-that-wont-replace-photography/)). Mango "trained a generative AI model" on real photos of each garment ([Mango](https://mangofashiongroup.com/en/w/mango-crea-la-primera-campa%C3%B1a-generada-con-inteligencia-artificial-para-su-l%C3%ADnea-teen)). Svedka/Silverside had one team train "AI models on character design" and another build world/environment models ([Comfy](https://comfy.org/customers/svedka-silverside/)).
- **Classic 3D pipeline + AI.** Lu do Magalu: 3D model for Unreal Engine, motion capture, real-time animation, AI-assisted production ([Magalu](https://ri.magazineluiza.com.br/Download.aspx?Arquivo=SaqC4WBAGPXST31V62qWtA%3D%3D)).
- **Manual finishing and storytelling.** Aitana's team uses Photoshop and plans her week; "people follow lives, not images" ([Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/next/2024/12/27/meet-the-first-spanish-ai-model-earning-up-to-10000-per-month)).

## 4. Dropshipping: evidence vs. speculation

### What sources show (evidence)

1. **Tools are built for and sold to dropshippers.** Zendrop, a dropshipping supplier platform, sells an "AI Ad Generator": pick a Zendrop product, "choose an avatar, pick a script", get an "AI UGC-style ad" for TikTok, Reels and Shorts, "without hiring creators" (K61). This proves supply and marketing to dropshippers, not usage volume.
2. **AI "UGC" actor platforms serve e-commerce.** Arcads advertises 1,000+ AI actors and custom AI actors and lists DTC customers (K60). TikTok itself offers avatar ads with licensed actors or AI-generated avatars and labels every export "AI-generated" (K57, K58).
3. **AI spokespersons sell products at scale, documented by journalists.** The New York Times (July 2026, relayed by Futurism and NutraIngredients) found hundreds of AI-generated wellness personas (fake doctors, "Eastern medicine" experts, enthusiastic "customers") selling supplements on Facebook, Instagram and TikTok; one unnamed Chinese firm claims 1,200 AI ad videos per day for TikTok brands at about $10 each, including a non-health gadget ad (K62).
4. **Affiliate/TikTok Shop model with AI avatars.** 404 Media (30 Jul 2026) reports a lawsuit by Humann alleging that Ambrosia/Rosabella coached creators via Discord to make AI "doctor" TikTok Shop videos, paid by commission; creators' tutorials show Veo 3 and HeyGen (K63, K64). These are allegations; Ambrosia disputes inducing the conduct.
5. **Licensed real-person avatars end up in dubious product ads.** AFP (Apr 2025): actor Simon Lee's avatar appeared as a gynaecologist or surgeon promoting unproven remedies on TikTok and Instagram (K66).
6. **Rules exist.** TikTok ad policy (Apr 2026) requires AIGC disclosure and rejects or restricts undisclosed AI ads (K59). The US FTC rule (Aug 2024) bans fake testimonials "by someone who does not exist, such as AI-generated fake reviews" (K67).

### What is not shown (speculation, keep off slides or label as hypothesis)

- No source quantifies what share of dropshipping stores or ads use AI spokespersons or AI "UGC".
- No source documents a specific named account as a fully AI-run dropshipping operation. Rosabella is a supplement brand selling through TikTok Shop affiliates; the only dropshipping link is a researcher's remark that "All these guys are ex-dropshipping guys" (K65).
- Scam-ad research (Gen Digital, 14.57M Meta ads, 31% pointing to scam infrastructure) does not break out AI-generated creatives or dropshipping (K68).
- Vendor pages show intended use, not results; their performance numbers (views, revenue) are unaudited.

Safe phrasing for the talk: "Dropshipping platforms now sell 'pick an AI face, get an ad' tools, and journalists have documented AI fake doctors and fake customers selling products at scale. How common this is among dropshippers has not been measured."
